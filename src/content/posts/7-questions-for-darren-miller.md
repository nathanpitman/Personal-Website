Some 22 years ago Darren Miller was kind enough to take part in a short interview series that I ran on my blog. In the time that has come since Darren and I were fortunate enough to work together, both in a self employed capacity (on a number of interesting projects) and as employees at iHasco, where we both helped shape the platform that would carry the business towards acquisition in 2020.

Looking back on the 10 questions which I posed to Darren and other participants in 2004, I thought it would be fun to revisit this series and see what, if anything had changed for him, how he reflects on the time that has passed since and what advice he'd offer today on "Going it Alone".

## In hindsight

**Nathan:** You answered 10 questions for me way back in 2005. Reflecting on that interview today, what does that version of you get right and what do you think you could have been naive about?

**Darren:**

> The freedom it gave me is definitely the highlight. I'm still not sure I ever charged enough.  
> It's interesting to read back that I didn't have a particular ambition to stick at programming. The opposite has turned out to be true - I get more enjoyment from it than ever before. Probably because I'm better at it.  
> I try not to dwell on the fact that I was theoretically capable of creating one of those apps that sold for billions.

## Where are you now?

**Nathan:** Are you still going it alone? If so, did this give you what you wanted or has life taken you in a direction you could never have anticipated way back then?

**Darren:**

> I lasted about 15 years solo before joining you at iHasco, which was a fun few years.  
> It was my lack of sales skill that ultimately sent me back to employment. That and the diminishing tax benefits of running a solo operation.  
> Today I am recently installed heading up the development team at Dr Fox online pharmacy.

## The highlights reel

**Nathan:** Reflecting on the last 20+ years, what are some of the moments that you're most proud of, or that have genuinely stuck with you as memorable experiences?

**Darren:**

> I had some fantastic travel experiences and freedom to spend time with my daughter when she was tiny. That would not have happened as an employee.  
> As a freelancer it was fun being along for the ride and seeing the industry change so much. I got to experience a much wider variety of projects than I would have in-house.  
> At iHasco it was great to be involved in the acquisition, the energy of the young team was quite infectious. Although the resulting corporate world was not my tempo.  
> My new role feels like it's landed just at the right time as I can bring everything I've learned to bear.

## The old neighbourhood

**Nathan:** The early web had a real sense of community - blogs, forums, CSS Zen Garden, web-rings and early Twitter (Sad emoji!). What we once knew has changed enormously but there are signs that people are seeking this out again, even if it's partly nostalgic. Do you feel the sense that a web community is returning, and what of that matters to you?

**Darren:**

> I'm not active on social media although I passively follow a few tech influencers. YouTube is the most interesting place these days, everything else became a culture war.

## Shifting sands

**Nathan:** The web industry has changed beyond all recognition since 2005. What's been the single biggest shift for you professionally and where does AI fit into that? Do you use it in your workflow, and regardless… do you think it changes something more fundamental about what it means to make things today?

**Darren:**

> It's all about AI now. The last 6 months have felt like a golden age. As a developer who knows what he wants, the ability to talk in plain English to a machine and steer it towards an outcome so quickly is like magic.  
> Who knows where it goes next though. In just the last week the increase in token costs has changed things significantly. Running multiple agents all day long now needs to justify it's cost. That "I wonder what happens..." side project just got expensive.  
> Hopefully the huge amount of competition will sort that out as there's no going back.

## The great pause

**Nathan:** COVID was something none of us could ever have anticipated when we were all going it alone back in the noughties. It reshaped how the whole world thought about work and life overnight. More importantly, it made open discussion about mental health and wellbeing acceptable. Did the pandemic change anything fundamental for you?

**Darren:**

> I was always working remotely and flexibly so it felt like everyone else catching up. I don't love video calls but it's better than commuting in.

## Tip please

**Nathan:** If someone came to you today and said they were thinking about "going it alone", how would your advice differ, knowing everything that you've learnt in the intervening period?

**Darren:**

> Having a stash of cash is probably still the most important thing.
